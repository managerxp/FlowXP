## Web app (inside FlowXP)
The FlowBook screens live in `frontend/src/app/books/` and render at `/app/books/*` inside the FlowXP app shell (FlowXP session, dark mode and business switcher); the API is mounted at `/api/books`. Run `backend` and `frontend`; there is no separate web server. A small `router.jsx` shim keeps page paths like `/sales`. Standalone `flowbook/api` (`server.ts`) still has password sign-in for dev and tests only.
Screens: sign in and sign up, set up a business, home (what is owed, profit, books check), sales invoices, purchase bills and credit notes (draft, issue, void, apply, print), payments with matching, customers, vendors, items, chart of accounts, journals (manual entry, reverse), reports (profit and loss, balance sheet, trial balance, who owes you / you owe, checks), settings (business details, GST rates, closing periods, team, FlowXP link with one-time secret and event replay, activity log). Not yet: sharing the FlowXP login, PDF download (Print uses the browser), bank statements, GST returns.

### Look and feel
The web app uses FlowXP's design system (`design.md`): same tokens (ink navy, logo blue, flat white grounds), Plus Jakarta Sans, 8/12/16 radii, borders over shadows, the same sidebar-plus-sticky-header shell, light and dark by the system setting. Motion is the FlowXP set: a short fade-and-rise on each page, staggered stat tiles, a lift on button hover, panels that settle in; all off under reduced motion.

### Hardening (2026-10-29)
Invoice, bill, credit note and payment numbers restart each financial year (`INV-26-27-000001`, 16 characters, within the GST limit); the invoice prefix is set per organization (Settings, up to three characters) and each prefix is its own series. Ageing is worked out for the date chosen. Posted payments and their allocations are frozen by database triggers (void instead). FlowXP events are stored and answered at once and posted by a background worker (`INBOX_WORKER=false` to post inside the request instead); a cancellation that arrives before its sale waits and runs by itself when the sale posts. Organizations made before the Discount given account existed got it from migration 0005.

### GST returns, recurring documents and statements (2026-10-29)
Added after reading the MIT-licensed [Free-GST-Billing-Software](https://github.com/IamRamgarhia/Free-GST-Billing-Software) (copyright its authors; the GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B file layout, the portal checks and the number rules follow it, re-written for FlowBook's paise and ledger model).
- **GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B drafts** (`api/src/gstr.ts`, Reports, GST returns): worked out from issued invoices, credit notes and bills of a month. GSTR-1 has B2B, B2CL (above 1 lakh from 1 Aug 2024), B2CS, CDNR, the HSN summary and documents issued. A list of what the portal is known to reject comes with it (invalid GSTIN, number over 16 characters, rate not on the portal list, missing or short HSN, missing unit), and the download is blocked until the blocking ones are fixed. **Not verified against the GST portal.** Not built: credit notes to unregistered buyers (CDNUR), exports, SEZ, reverse charge, cess, GSTR-3B table 3.2, interest and late fee, filing.
- **Units (UQC)** on items and lines (migration 0006); the HSN table needs them.
- **Recurring invoices and bills** (`api/src/recurring.ts`, migration 0006, Recurring screen): a template makes a DRAFT on each due date (catches up missed periods, at most 12 per run, stops at its end date). A person still issues it. It runs when someone presses "Make due drafts now"; there is no timer yet.
- **Party statement** (`api/src/statement.ts`, Reports, Party statement): documents and payments with a running balance.
- **Amount in words** under every document total, and a **GST due dates** card on the home screen (monthly filers only).
- Left out on purpose: the reference's HSN-to-rate table. Its rates predate or only partly follow the September 2025 rate change, so it would suggest wrong rates. Seeded tax codes are still GST0/5/12/18/28; add a 40% code (and retire 12/28 for new dates) in Settings once the owner's CA confirms the rates in use.

### WhatsApp (2026-10-29)
Settings, WhatsApp. Two modes, plus off:
- **Open WhatsApp (LINK):** no setup. The Send and Remind buttons on an invoice, and Send receipt on a payment, open a click-to-chat address with the message ready; a person presses send.
- **Send from FlowBook (CLOUD):** FlowBook sends through the business's own Meta WhatsApp Cloud API number (`api/src/whatsapp.ts`), and can remind on its own: an hourly worker (`WHATSAPP_WORKER=false` turns it off) sends the unpaid-invoice reminder at the chosen days from the due date (default 3 days before, on the day, 7 and 15 days after). Each invoice gets each reminder once, only while money is pending.
- Needs three **Utility** templates approved in Meta Business Manager, named as in Settings, with exactly the text shown there (`TEMPLATE_BODY` in whatsapp.ts). The access token is stored encrypted (AES-256-GCM); set `FLOWBOOK_SECRET_KEY` (32 random bytes, base64) in `api/.env` first or saving a token is refused. Inside the FlowXP API the key is derived from FlowXP's `ENCRYPTION_KEY`/`JWT_SECRET`, and a business that has no Meta account of its own sends from FlowXP's WhatsApp number (the templates must be approved on that number).
- A customer or vendor can be marked "do not send WhatsApp" and is then skipped (and logged as skipped). Every message, sent or not, is in the log on the invoice and in Settings.
- **Not tested against WhatsApp's live service** (needs a real number and approved templates); tests use a stand-in sender. No delivery or read receipts yet (needs Meta's webhook), no reply handling.

### Expenses and CSV import (2026-10-29)
- **Expenses** (`api/src/expenses.ts`, Expenses screen): money spent and paid at once. One journal: the expense account and the GST you can claim back are debited, cash, bank, UPI or card is credited. It can be voided (reversed). The GST on expenses counts as credit in the GSTR-3B draft. Something paid later is a purchase bill. No receipt attachments yet, and the vendor is only a name in the note (not a party).
- **CSV import** (`api/src/import.ts`, `web/src/csv.js`, Import CSV on Customers, Vendors and Items): the browser reads the file, "Check" runs the real import and undoes it so the preview is exact, then "Import" saves the good rows and lists the rest with the line number. Columns are matched by name in any order (template download included); a unit can be a code or a name, GST can be `18` or `GST18`, a state can be a name or code. Up to 1000 rows per file. Opening balances are not imported.

### Printing, e-way bill, TDS/TCS and sales analysis (2026-10-29)
- **Business profile and printing** (`api/src/profile.ts`, migration 0008, Settings > Business and Printing): address, phone, email, bank, IFSC, UPI and terms; print choices (A4, A5 or 80 mm thermal, colour, heading, show HSN/unit/bank/terms). Customers and vendors have a billing and a delivery address. Press Print on an issued invoice, bill or credit note, or "Print receipt" on a payment: a separate sheet (`web/src/pages/Print.jsx`) is shown only on paper, with CGST/SGST or IGST, TCS, amount in words, bank/UPI, terms and a signature line.
- **E-way bill file** (`api/src/eway.ts`, "E-way bill" on an issued invoice): asks for distance, mode, vehicle and transporter, lists what is missing (GSTIN, PIN codes), and downloads the bulk-upload JSON. It is a file to upload, not an e-way bill, and has not been checked on the NIC portal.
- **TDS** (migration 0009, payment form): tax a customer deducted is kept as TDS receivable; tax you deduct from a vendor is TDS payable. The payment amount stays the cash that moved; cash plus TDS settles the documents. The statement and the books both use the full amount.
- **TCS** (invoice form): a percent of value plus GST, added to the total and owed as TCS payable; invoices only. Rates and sections shown in the pickers are a helper, not tax advice.
- **Sales analysis** (Reports): month-by-month sales and purchases, top customers with what they still owe, best-selling items.
- **Tax-inclusive prices:** tick "GST is in this price" on a line; the tax is carved out of what you typed, so the total is exactly that amount (migration 0010).
- **Opening balances** (Settings > Opening balances, migration 0011): what customers owed you and you owed vendors when you started. Posted against an "Opening balances" account, payable like a normal document, shown on statements, and kept out of GST returns and sales analysis.
- **Reverse charge** (tick it on a purchase bill, migration 0012): the vendor is owed the price only; the GST is credited as output tax and debited as input credit, so it nets to nothing in the books. GSTR-3B shows it in 3.1(d) and as credit. Not covered: self-invoices, payment vouchers, and rules on which supplies are eligible; your CA decides when to use it.
- **Bank reconciliation** (Money > Bank, migration 0013, `api/src/reconcile.ts`): import the bank's CSV (one signed Amount column, or Deposit/Withdrawal columns; day-first dates) for a bank, UPI or card account; the same file twice adds nothing. Each statement line is matched one-to-one with an entry in the books (same amount; the nearest one within 5 days is suggested), or you add the missing entry (a charge, interest) and it matches itself, or ignore it. The screen shows the closing balance the statement should show, and the difference once you type the real one. Not built: bank feeds, rules that categorise lines automatically, matching one line to several entries, saved "reconciled up to" locks.
- **Quotes and sales orders** (Sales > Quotes, Sales orders; migration 0014): documents that post nothing and are never owed. "Mark as sent" numbers them (QUO-/SO-), the customer can be marked accepted or declined, and a sent quote becomes a sales order or an invoice, a sales order an invoice. The new document is a draft with the same lines and a "Made from" link; the original shows "Turned into". Not built: partial invoicing of an order, expiry reminders, emailing a quote, a customer accept link.
- **Purchase orders** (Purchases > Purchase orders, migration 0015): same as quotes: numbered PO-… when sent, posts nothing, vendor accepted or declined, and "Make bill" turns it into a draft bill with the same lines.
- **Debit notes** (Purchases > Debit notes, migration 0016): a vendor's bill reduced (goods returned, price corrected). Posts Dr Accounts payable, Cr Purchase returns and Cr the input GST that had been claimed; GSTR-3B credit is reduced to match. The credit sits on the note and "Apply to bills" sets it against the vendor's open bills (no new journal), like a credit note against invoices. It can refer to an issued bill of the same vendor, but not one under reverse charge. Vendor statements and the payables check include it.
- **Email** (Settings > Email, migrations 0017-0018): mail goes out through the platform's own mailbox (inside the FlowXP API that is FlowXP's own mailbox; on its own, set once in the server environment: `FLOWBOOK_SMTP_HOST`, `_PORT`, `_SECURE`, `_USER`, `_PASS`, `_FROM`), shown as "<business> via FlowBook" with Reply-To set to the business (Settings > Email, or the profile email). Businesses enter no mail login. "Send email" on issued invoices, credit notes, quotes, orders, debit notes and received-payment receipts puts the document in the message body (no PDF attachment yet), to the party's saved email or one you type, and logs every attempt (sent/failed). Not tested against a real mail server.
- **Stock** (Books > Stock, migration 0019): tick "Count its stock" on a goods item. A bill puts the price into Inventory and adds to the count; an invoice takes the count down at the weighted-average cost and posts a second entry, Dr Cost of goods sold / Cr Inventory; credit notes bring goods back at today's average, debit notes send them out at the price paid. Selling more than is on hand is refused. Opening stock, write-offs and found stock are adjustments against an account you choose; a low-stock flag shows at the item's reorder level. Voiding a document puts its moves back (a purchase that was partly sold cannot be voided). Every move is an append-only row, so quantity and value are plain sums and Inventory in the books equals stock value. Back-dated entries (migration 0026) are costed at the average as it stood on their own date, and when an earlier purchase or movement is entered the later sales are worked out again in date order; each correction is posted for the date it corrects (Inventory against Cost of goods sold) so the books keep equalling the stock. Stock can never go below zero on any day. Warehouses (0027): quantity per warehouse, a default one, transfers at the average with no journal, a warehouse on each document, and each warehouse cannot go below zero; cost stays one average per item across them. Batches and serial numbers (0028): an item is counted plainly, by batch with an expiry date (selling takes the earliest expiry first unless you choose) or by serial number (one per unit, none in stock twice); you name them when issuing the bill or invoice, and Stock shows what is in each batch, what expires soon and which serials are held. Cost is one weighted average per item, not per batch or warehouse; recosting can leave a paisa of rounding difference on split batches.
- **Multi-currency** (Settings > Currencies, migration 0020): invoices, bills, quotes and orders can be written in USD, EUR, GBP, AED and a dozen other two-decimal currencies. The books and GST stay in rupees: prices are converted at the document's rate (the latest rate saved on or before its date, or typed on the document) and the foreign total is kept beside it and shown on the page, print sheet and email. A payment settles one foreign document at a time: you give the part of the foreign amount it settles and the rupees that actually moved; the rupees the document was booked at for that share, minus the rupees that moved, is posted to Exchange gain or loss (Cr for a gain, Dr for a loss), and voiding the payment restores both balances. Credit and debit notes, rounding off and TDS are rupee-only; a rupee payment cannot be allocated to a foreign document. Revaluation (migration 0025, Settings > Currencies): restates open foreign invoices and bills at a date's rate, posts the unrealised difference to the same exchange gain or loss account and reverses it the next day (finished later if that day's period is not open yet). Not done: a separate export table in GSTR-1, foreign-currency bank accounts, and zero-decimal currencies such as JPY.
- **Exports in GSTR-1** (migration 0030): an invoice in a foreign currency is always IGST and is listed in the `exp` table, `WPAY` when tax was charged and `WOPAY` (letter of undertaking) when not, with shipping bill number, date and port code entered on the invoice (warning if goods lack them). It is left out of B2B/B2CS and shown as zero-rated (3.1(b)) in GSTR-3B. Not done: SEZ supplies, deemed exports, CDNUR (credit notes to unregistered buyers) and export credit notes (credit notes are rupee-only). Unverified on the GST portal.
- **Foreign-currency bank accounts** (`bankacct.ts`, migration 0031; Bank page > Your accounts): add a bank account in rupees or a currency. A foreign account's journal lines carry the amount in its own currency beside the rupee value (`journal_lines.foreign_amount_minor`); the ledger refuses a posting to it without that amount. Buying dollars (rupees to the account) carries them at the rupees paid; selling or paying out takes them out at the account's average rupee value (`holding`/`costOfForeign` in fx.ts) and the difference from what they fetched or settled is the realised exchange gain or loss. A customer payment or vendor payment of a foreign invoice or bill can go through the matching account (`accountId`, optional `rate`, the rupees follow). Revaluation also restates these accounts (reversing next day, ignored by the booked value). Bank reconciliation of a foreign account is in its own currency; "Add entry" posts at the day's rate (deposit) or the average (withdrawal). Transfers between your accounts: `/bank/transfers` (rupees to dollars, dollars to rupees, same currency between accounts; two different foreign currencies must go through rupees), voidable. Not done: bank charges taken out of the foreign amount of a document payment, FIFO cost, foreign cash/petty accounts in expenses, per-account statement import of other currencies' formats.
- **Fixed assets** (Books > Fixed assets, migration 0021): a register of assets with cost, scrap value and a method: straight line over a life in years (the last month takes the rounding so cost minus scrap is exactly written off), written-down value at a yearly percent (a twelfth each month), or none (land). Buying posts Dr Fixed assets against Bank, Cash, payables or, for something the business already had, opening balances (with depreciation already taken; it is dated on the first day of the books). "Post depreciation" posts every month not yet done, one entry per month (Dr Depreciation / Cr Accumulated depreciation), counting the start month as a full month, and is safe to repeat. Selling or writing off clears cost and accumulated depreciation and posts the difference to Gain or loss on sale of assets; depreciation must be up to date through the month before. An asset entered by mistake can be cancelled until depreciation is posted. GST on a sale (migration 0024): pick a GST code when selling; it is charged on top of the price, credited to output tax and included in GSTR-3B outward supplies for that month. Yearly views (`assets-tax.ts`, migration 0029; Fixed assets > Year by year, `GET /assets/schedule?fy=` and `/assets/tax-depreciation?fy=`, fy = year the April-March year starts in): the book schedule per asset (cost and accumulated depreciation at start, added, sold, at end, worth at end; each year closes into the next), and the Income Tax Act block schedule, a report that posts nothing. Each asset can be given a tax block (11 standard blocks with fixed rates in `IT_BLOCKS`; set when adding or from the list, `PATCH /assets/:id/tax`); the block's depreciation is the rate on opening value plus additions, additions used under 180 days get half rate, sale proceeds come off the block, and a block sold out or driven below zero ends as a short-term capital gain or loss instead of depreciation. An older asset takes its tax value at the start of the financial year the books begin in. Not done: additional depreciation (sec 32(1)(iia)), rates that change with acquisition date (such as certain vehicles), tax versus book difference/deferred tax, asset categories with their own book rates.
- **Projects** (Sales > Projects, migration 0022): a project has a customer, an hourly rate and an optional budget. Log time as 1.5 or 1:30 (billable or not, with the rate fixed at the moment it is logged); "Invoice the hours" turns all unbilled billable time into a draft invoice (one line per entry, in hours) and marks it billed, and deleting or voiding that invoice frees the hours again. Invoices, bills, credit and debit notes carry an optional project, and expenses can be tagged too. The project page shows hours, still-to-bill, invoiced (before GST, less credit notes), bills and expenses (less debit notes, cancelled expenses ignored), profit and budget left. Not done: timers, per-task rates, staff timesheets and approvals.
- **Free trial** (migration 0032, `app.ts` `scope()`): a new business starts on plan `TRIAL` with `trial_ends_at` seven days out (businesses that existed before are `ACTIVE`). After that date reads and exports still work but any write returns 402 `trial_ended`; `/v1/me` carries `plan`, `trial_ends_at`, `trial_days_left` and the app shows a banner. Switching a business on is manual for now: `UPDATE organizations SET plan = 'ACTIVE' WHERE id = ...`. **One sign-in, no FlowBook password** (superseded 2026-10-30): FlowBook is mounted in the FlowXP API at `/api/books` behind FlowXP's own session; `sso.ts` `hostSession()` finds or creates the FlowBook user and books for the signed-in FlowXP user and business (no token, no secret, no handoff) and copies plan/trial from the business. Business type `FLOWBOOK` can use it; paying businesses with the `flowbook` plan feature too. Not built: payment, plan upgrade screen, trial-ending emails, member invites from FlowXP.
- **E-invoicing** (`einvoice.ts`, migration 0034; invoice and credit note pages > E-invoice, GST returns > E-invoices): for an issued invoice or credit note to a customer with a GSTIN, FlowBook builds the IRP file in the published schema (version 1.1: `TranDtls`, `DocDtls`, seller, buyer, `ItemList`, `ValDtls`, `PrecDocDtls` for a credit note) and lists exactly what is missing (business or customer address, city, PIN, HSN of 4-8 digits, number format, credit note's invoice, totals that do not add up). `GET /einvoice?from&to` lists each document as registered, ready, needs fixing or not required; `GET /einvoice/bulk` is one array file for all ready ones; `GET /einvoice/:id/file` one file. You upload on the IRP (portal or your GSP), then record the answer (`POST /einvoice/:id/irn`: 64-hex IRN, acknowledgement number and date, optional signed QR); recording a cancellation is `POST /einvoice/:id/cancel`, and a document with a live IRN cannot be voided until then. FlowBook does not call the IRP, does not compute or validate the IRN itself, does not draw the QR on the print sheet, and has not checked the file on the portal. Not covered: exports and SEZ, reverse charge, e-commerce and transport blocks, deciding whether your turnover requires e-invoicing, and putting the IRN into GSTR-1.
